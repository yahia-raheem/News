Saqr Al-Nour states, “The challenges facing desert reclamation projects stem not just from water scarcity, but also from the way it is managed and allocated. Intensive aquifer extraction leads to a sustained decline in the water table, disproportionately affecting local residents and small-scale farmers, whose wells are typically shallow, often only tens of meters deep. Conversely, large-scale investors can maintain operations by utilizing deeper wells and more advanced pumping technologies.” He also highlights another significant issue: there are no national maps indicating the amount of water that can be safely extracted. So people are draining the aquifer blindly, with no real sense of its limits and capacity. There aren’t even maps marking out danger zones—no red, yellow, or green areas to guide water use, which are vital for effective water management in dry countries. For Saqr, this lack of a clear, scientific foundation means no one really knows where farming is feasible and where it should be halted.
