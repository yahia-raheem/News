2014–2024:
Egyptian Countryside Leads Reclamation, Private Sector Follows

Analysis of data reveals a significant decline in the prominence of agricultural cooperatives, which held a significant position in certain years leading up to 2018, within the land reclamation system. Their contribution has decreased substantially from 73.6% in 2017 and 69.3% in 2018 to 1.49% in 2024. This reduction is closely associated with a re-evaluation of the cooperatives' function within the land reclamation framework, rather than shifts in demand or production levels.

The increasing prominence of the NECDC and the National Service Projects Organization suggests a shift in governmental strategies. The NECDC, established in 2016, progressively increased its involvement to encompass 62.5% of all land reclamation activities by 2024. Concurrently, the National Service Projects Organization's participation peaked at 44.8% in 2023, subsequently stabilizing at 20.3% in 2024. These developments suggest that the diminished role of the private sector and cooperatives is not necessarily a permanent trend. Instead, it reflects a deliberate reallocation of state-sponsored projects to specific organizations in recent years. Consequently, forecasting future percentage distributions is of limited value, given the ongoing changes in policies and implementing bodies.

2007–2013:
Cooperative Associations Lead Land Reclamation

Prior to 2014, agricultural land reclamation exhibited a markedly different trend than it does at present. From 2007 to 2013, agricultural cooperative societies were the primary drivers of reclamation projects. Notably, in 2007, these societies accounted for approximately 97% of all reclaimed land, while the private sector contributed only 3%. This disparity diminished over time, and by 2013, the private sector's involvement had grown substantially, approaching the scale of cooperative initiatives. In that year, cooperatives managed 52.6% of reclamation efforts, compared to the private sector's 45%. The General Authority for Reconstruction Projects and Agricultural Development maintained a consistently minor role throughout this period, with its cumulative share remaining below 2-3% of total reclamation activities.
