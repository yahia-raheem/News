Back in the late 1950s, Gamal Abdel Nasser rolled out the “Parallel Valley” plan. It was bold—he wanted to create a whole new valley running alongside the Nile, stretching through the Western Desert from Aswan all the way to the Bahariya Oasis, and covering places like Dakhla, Kharga, and Farafra. The goal was to reclaim three million acres and change Egypt’s landscape for good. But the dream was bigger than what Egypt could actually pull off. Money and technical problems kept piling up. Over time, the “Parallel Valley” turned into more of a symbol—a big hope that kept getting passed from one government to the next, but never actually happened.
