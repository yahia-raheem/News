A study published in 2020, "Soil Salinity Mapping and Water Quality Assessment in the Siwa Oasis Using Geographic Information Systems," corroborates these findings. The study indicated that newly reclaimed land is primarily located on the periphery of the Siwa Oasis, particularly in the elevated regions, owing to the relative ease of land reclamation in these areas compared to those closer to the lake.

Spatial maps from 1992, 2002, and 2015 demonstrate a notable increase in agricultural land cover, with expansion concentrated in the central and eastern sectors of the oasis. Conversely, the northwestern region, proximal to Lake Siwa, experienced an increase in inundated areas resulting from inadequate drainage and intensive irrigation practices. These previously wet sabkhas have undergone transformation into waterlogged terrains. Concurrently, the area of flooded land expanded by approximately 33 square kilometers, constituting 5.4% of the total oasis area by 2015. The saline area also expanded by roughly 29 square kilometers, encompassing over 10% of the oasis.
