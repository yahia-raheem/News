He recounted that his farm once sustained a diverse range of trees, including citrus fruits, oranges, guavas, pomegranates, palm trees, and olives. However, beginning in 2013–2014, he started to observe the first signs of change: the water's taste altered, and subsequently, the fruits of the land were affected. Dates shrank in size, and overall productivity declined. Gradually, the more sensitive trees perished, leaving only pomegranates, palm trees, and olives, which were also impacted by the deteriorating, increasingly saline water quality.

Haider attributes this shift in water quality to three interrelated factors: the increase in arable land; the influx of investors utilizing deep drilling and aggressive pumping techniques; the pressure on ancient Roman wells in the farming region; and the agricultural drainage dilemma, which has led to the accumulation of saline surface water in lakes, resulting in a rise in groundwater levels and surface land salinity. "Without proper drainage treatment, Siwa has no future," remarks Haider.
