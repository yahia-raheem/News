A 2021 study published in Springer Nature by researchers Muhammad El-Tarabili and Hossam El-Din El-Moghazi, titled "GIS-Based Assessment and Statistical Identification of Groundwater Geochemistry for Potential Irrigation Use in Al-Maghra, Egypt," assessed the suitability of groundwater for irrigation purposes. The study involved a geochemical analysis of 46 groundwater samples collected during deep well drilling in 2018 and 2019, covering an area of 230,000 acres.

The research concluded that approximately 25% of the groundwater within the project area is unsuitable for irrigation due to its excessive hardness and a magnesium risk exceeding 50%. Furthermore, crop classification based on electrical conductivity indicated that only halophytic and highly salt-tolerant crops could survive if the groundwater is used for irrigation without prior treatment.
