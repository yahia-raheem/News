Water Well Salinity

Alaa El-Din's farm, typical of the region, utilizes an underground well extending to a depth of 195.5 meters to extract approximately 1,500 cubic meters of water daily for irrigation. Alaa El-Din independently financed the well's construction, as not all farms provided by the NECDC included pre-existing water wells.

The well is connected to the Al-Maghra Aquifer, which spans from the western edge of the Nile Delta to the east of the Qattara Depression. The aquifer's thickness varies from 50 meters in the east to 900 meters in the west. The aquifer's water is a combination of ancient water in the west and renewable water in the east. According to a 2019 study by the Egyptian Desalination Research Center of Excellence (affiliated with the Ministry of Agriculture and Land Reclamation), the total groundwater salinity within this basin ranges from 1,000 parts per million in the east to 20,000 parts per million at the Qattara Depression in the west.

Dr. Ahmad Fawzy Diab, a United Nations water strategy expert and Professor of Water Resources at the Desert Research Center, notes that water salinity in Al-Maghra varies with depth. The first and second layers exhibit salinity levels below 4,000 parts per million. However, salinity increases with extraction depth, ranging from 5,000 to 20,000 parts per million, rendering it suitable only for salt-tolerant plant species.
