It’s 1997, and the heat in the Toshka desert is almost unbearable. Hosni Mubarak, Egypt’s former president, stands in front of a crowd of cameras and declares he’s going to “turn this stretch of desert into a green paradise.” He promises to move Egyptian farming beyond the crowded banks of the Nile and out into the open desert. For a lot of people, that moment stuck—it became the image of what land reclamation meant. But the truth is, this wasn’t where the story started.
