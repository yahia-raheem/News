He further stated that Fayoum's natural topography as a closed depression, coupled with its reliance on the Bahr Yusuf canal as its sole water source, inevitably leads to salinity accumulation due to the lack of water replenishment. He asserted that “the inherent limitations of Fayoum's water resources preclude the sustainable reclamation of new lands.” He also noted that agricultural reclamation efforts, particularly those lacking proper planning, have excessively depleted water reserves, negatively impacting existing agricultural lands through increased salinity and reduced productivity.

He further noted that the inherently high salinity of the reclaimed sandy soils in Sayyidna Musa and Sayyidna al-Khidr is further aggravated by wastewater irrigation. He elaborated that elevated salinity diminishes land productivity and consequently doubles financial losses for farmers.
