She envisioned a future where she could cultivate wheat, clover, and corn to provide for her family and livestock, finally free from working other people’s fields. But then she faced the saline water. No wheat. No clover. No vegetables. Just olive trees, hanging on through each dry season, barely enough to pay the cooperative what she owes for the land.

So now, Nabila owns land in name only. She spends her days working on someone else’s farm. She is out the door before 7:00 AM, standing in the heat until 3:00 PM, earning 200 Egyptian pounds—that’s about four dollars—per day. When she gets home, she still has more domestic tasks waiting for her. Even after open-heart surgery, her body refuses to give her a break. Nothing about her health changes the bitter truth for women in her village: either work all day under the burning sun, or go hungry.
