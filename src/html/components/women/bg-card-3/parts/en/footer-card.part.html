A 2024 study in the Hydrogeology Journal, "Towards Sustainable Groundwater Management in Egypt's Deserts," reveals that a majority of agricultural initiatives in the Western Desert predominantly utilize non-renewable groundwater resources, specifically the Nubian Sandstone Aquifer and the Maghra Aquifer. The research demonstrates a marked reduction in groundwater levels across the Farafra, Dakhla, Kharga, and East Oweinat regions in recent decades, attributable to extensive pumping for agricultural purposes. Furthermore, it identifies a substantial rise in salinity and a deterioration of water quality in areas like Wadi El Natrun and Maghra, stemming from excessive extraction and the concentration of irrigation runoff. These trends pose a threat to the sustainability of new agricultural projects and the soil’s productive capacity.
